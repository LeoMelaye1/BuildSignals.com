Why tub-to-shower is worth its own campaign

Pulling out a tub that nobody uses and putting in a bigger, easier shower is one of the most common bathroom changes homeowners make right now. The 2026 U.S. Houzz Bathroom Trends Study, a survey of 1,619 U.S. homeowners renovating a bathroom, found that a bathtub is removed in 26% of projects, and when a tub comes out, 79% of those homeowners use the space to enlarge the shower. The same study puts median spending on bathroom upgrades at $15,000 nationally (Houzz).

For a remodeler, conversions have a few things going for them:

  • A clear problem. The homeowner already knows what they want gone: a tub that is hard to step over, rarely used, or worn out.
  • A defined scope. Most jobs stay inside the same footprint, so you can quote quickly and schedule tightly.
  • A fast decision. Compared with a full kitchen, a conversion is a smaller decision, and many homeowners are ready to pick a contractor at the first visit.
  • Repeatable crews. The same crew and the same product line can do several a week, which keeps your margins steady.

Tub-to-shower leads vs. booked conversion appointments

A "lead" is a name and a phone number. A booked appointment is a qualified homeowner who is expecting you at a set time. The difference matters most for conversions, because the homeowner who asked about a new shower on Tuesday night is often talking to someone else by Wednesday afternoon.

That is why BuildSignals calls every new lead within minutes, not hours. The faster call is the cheapest way to win more conversions from the same ad spend. More on that in speed to lead for remodeling contractors.

Close more of the conversions you run

  • Bring samples. Wall panels, tile, glass and base options. Homeowners pick faster when they can touch them.
  • Measure and photograph the tub area. Valve location, window, wall condition. It speeds up the quote and shows you know the job.
  • Talk about safety without making it awkward. A low threshold, a seat and grab bars are easy add-ons that many older buyers want but will not ask for.
  • Give a price and a start date at the table. Conversions are often decided at the first visit.
  • Follow up the ones who wait. A short call a few days later wins jobs that would otherwise go quiet.
