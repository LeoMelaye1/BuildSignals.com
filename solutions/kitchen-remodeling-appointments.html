Why kitchen leads need different handling than bathroom leads

Kitchens are the most common room homeowners renovate. In the 2026 Houzz & Home Study, 26% of renovating homeowners worked on a kitchen. The jobs are also big. The 2026 U.S. Houzz Kitchen Trends Study (1,780 homeowners) puts the median major kitchen remodel at $55,000: $46,000 for a smaller kitchen and $75,000 for a larger one. Minor kitchen projects land at $20,000.

That size changes how you should buy and work leads:

  • Longer decisions. A $55,000 project usually involves two decision makers, a design conversation and more than one visit. A lead that is not called fast and booked with both owners present tends to stall.
  • Wider budget spread. A homeowner who wants new cabinet doors and one who wants to move walls both say "kitchen remodel." If nobody asks about scope and budget before the visit, you drive out to quote jobs you do not do.
  • A shared lead costs more than it looks. When the same kitchen inquiry goes to several companies, the homeowner gets several calls and often picks whoever reached them first or quoted lowest. With a job this size, losing that race hurts.

Close more of the kitchen estimates you run

A booked appointment is only worth what you close from it. A few habits help on bigger kitchen jobs:

  • Bring photos of finished kitchens from your own jobs, close in size to theirs.
  • Show two or three scope options at different price points instead of one number.
  • Set the next step before you leave: a design meeting, a showroom visit or a measured plan.
  • Follow up on open estimates for weeks, not days. Kitchen decisions take time.
