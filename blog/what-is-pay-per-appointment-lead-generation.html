What Is Pay-Per-Appointment Lead Generation? A Guide for Kitchen and Bath Remodelers

By Leo Melaye, founder of BuildSignals · October 2, 2026

Pay-per-appointment lead generation means you pay for booked, qualified appointments instead of clicks, leads, or a monthly retainer. For a remodeler, that's the closest thing to paying for an actual shot at a job.

Leads vs. appointments

A lead is a name and a phone number. Many never answer, some are renters, some just wanted a price. An appointment is a homeowner who has talked to someone, confirmed they want the work, confirmed a budget, and agreed to a time for an in-home estimate. Paying per appointment moves the work of chasing and qualifying leads off your plate.

What should count as a qualified appointment

  • Homeowner: not a renter or a tenant asking for a landlord.
  • Real project: they want the remodel, not just a ballpark number.
  • Budget confirmed: they've confirmed a budget that fits the work before you drive out.
  • Decision-makers: everyone who signs off on the job will be there.
  • Confirmed: the appointment is confirmed before you go.

Get this definition in writing before you start. It's the single biggest difference between agencies.

How billing usually works

You agree on a price per appointment for your market. Appointments get booked on your calendar, and you're billed for what was delivered. At BuildSignals, your card is charged once a week for that week's appointments. No retainer, no long-term contract, and you can stop when it stops working for you.

What the agency should be doing behind the scenes

  1. Running ads to homeowners in your service area, usually on Facebook and Instagram with lead forms built to filter out tire-kickers.
  2. Calling fast. The first few minutes after someone fills out a form matter most. See our post on speed to lead.
  3. Qualifying each lead against the definition above.
  4. Booking and confirming the appointment on your calendar.
  5. Reporting every lead and appointment so you can see what you're paying for.

Red flags

  • No written definition of a qualified appointment.
  • The agency owns the ad account and won't give you access.
  • Long contracts or big setup fees before you've seen a single appointment.
  • No real results from contractors like you.
